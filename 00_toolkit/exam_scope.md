# Exam Scope: What Can and Can't Come Up

Source: *WSET Level 3 Award in Wines Specification*, Issue 2 (May 2022) —
`06_past_papers/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf`.

WSET states plainly: **"All examination questions are based on the published
learning outcomes."** The Specification's *Ranges* tables are therefore the
literal boundary of the exam. If a region, grape, law term or fault is named in
a Range, it is examinable. If it isn't named, it is off-syllabus.

WSET does not release past papers, so the Ranges + the weighting tables below
are the closest thing to a definitive answer on what will come up.

---

## 1. Structure of the theory exam (Unit 1)

Closed book, **2 hours**, two parts:

| Part | Format | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 | 50 multiple-choice questions, 1 mark each | 50 |
| Part 2 | 4 short written answer questions, 25 marks each | 100 |

**Pass mark is 55% in *both* parts separately** — not the combined average. You
cannot carry a strong MCQ score into a weak written paper. There is a separate
tasting exam (Unit 2, 30 min, two blind wines) also needing 55%; both units must
be sat at the same sitting.

Marks are **not** subtracted for wrong answers in either part, so never leave a
blank.

## 2. Where the marks actually are

This is the part worth memorising. WSET publishes the exact weighting:

**Multiple choice — 50 marks**

| Learning Outcome | Topic | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| LO2 | **Still wine regions** | **28** |
| LO1 | Vineyard & winery factors | 8 |
| LO3 | Sparkling wine | 5 |
| LO4 | Fortified wine | 5 |
| LO5 | Service, faults, pairing, health | 4 |

**Short written answers — 100 marks**

| Learning Outcome | Topic | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| LO2 | **Still wine regions** | **70** |
| LO3 & LO4 | Sparkling + fortified combined | 20 |
| LO5 | Wine recommendations & service | 10 |
| LO1 | Assessed *within* all four questions, not scored separately | — |

**Regions are 56% of the MCQ and 70% of the written paper.** Everything else
combined is a minority of the exam. LO1 is never asked in isolation on the
written paper — it is the explanatory engine you apply *to* regions.

## 3. How the four written questions are built

The Specification fixes which outcomes each question draws on:

| Question | Learning Outcomes |
|---|---|
| 1 | LO1 & LO2 |
| 2 | LO1 & LO2 |
| 3 | LO1, LO2 & LO5 (Range 1 only) |
| 4 | LO1, LO3, LO4 & LO5 (Range 1 only) |

So: **Q1 and Q2 are pure still-wine region questions.** Q3 is a region question
with a service/recommendation tail. Q4 is the only place sparkling and fortified
appear on the written paper, and they share it with a service element.

Note "LO5 **Range 1 only**" — on the written paper, LO5 is restricted to *making
recommendations, storage and service*. Faults, food pairing and health issues
(LO5 Ranges 2–4) are examinable **in the MCQ only**.

Questions are multi-part with marks printed per part (the published sample splits
25 marks into 3+3+4 / 5+5+2+3). Match answer length to the mark allocation.

## 5. In-scope sparkling wines (LO3)

Champagne (Montagne de Reims, Côte des Blancs, Vallée de la Marne, Côte des Bar,
Côte de Sézanne); Crémant d'Alsace / de Bourgogne / de Loire; Saumur; Vouvray ·
**Cava** · **Asti**; **Prosecco** (+ Conegliano-Valdobbiadene) · **Deutscher
Sekt** · Tasmania; Yarra Valley; Adelaide Hills · Marlborough · **Cap Classique**
· Anderson Valley; Los Carneros

Production methods examinable: traditional, transfer, ancestral, tank, Asti,
carbonation. Sweetness law: Brut Nature, Brut, Demi-Sec.

## 6. In-scope fortified wines (LO4)

**Port** (Portugal) — Ruby (Reserve), Tawny (Reserve), LBV, Vintage, Tawny with
an Indication of Age. **Sherry** (Spain) — Fino, Amontillado, Manzanilla,
Oloroso, Palo Cortado, PX, Pale Cream, Medium, Cream. **Fortified Muscats** —
Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise (France), Rutherglen (Australia).

## 7. LO5 closed lists

- **Faults (MCQ only):** cork taint, oxidation, reduction, volatile acidity,
  *Brettanomyces*, out of condition, high sulfur dioxide. That is the whole list.
- **Wine considerations for pairing:** sweetness, acidity, tannin (oak),
  complexity, fruitiness, alcohol level.
- **Food considerations:** sweetness, acidity, umami, salt, bitterness, chilli
  heat, fat, flavour intensity.
- **Selection criteria:** wine style, wine quality, individual preferences and
  sensitivities, price, occasion.
- **Health:** excess consumption of wine only.

## 8. Coverage audit against this repo

Checked every region referenced in `web/index.html` against §4 above.

**No out-of-scope content.** All 25 regions currently used in the app are named
in the Specification, and the grape varieties attached to them (Furmint/Tokaj,
Malbec/Cahors, Monastrell/Jumilla, Zinfandel/Lodi, Touriga Nacional/Douro) all
appear in the relevant Range 2 rows.

**Gaps, ranked by exam value.** Given regions carry 70 of the 100 written marks,
these are where marks are being left on the table:

| Gap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Chile** — no coverage at all | Whole country in scope; Carmenère is a principal variety and a WSET "Essential" tasting sample |
| **South Africa** — no coverage at all | Whole country in scope; Pinotage + Chenin, Stellenbosch/Swartland all "Essential" |
| **Napa / Sonoma / Willamette / Columbia Valley** | USA is represented only by Lodi — one of the least significant AVAs on the list |
| **Northern Rhône** (Côte Rôtie, Hermitage, Condrieu, Cornas) | Only Châteauneuf-du-Pape is covered; premium Northern Rhône Syrah is "Essential" |
| **Veneto** (Valpolicella, Amarone, Ripasso, Soave) | Four "Essential" tastings, zero coverage |
| **Spain beyond Rioja + Jumilla** (Ribera del Duero, Rueda, Rías Baixas, Priorat) | All "Essential" |
| **Loire beyond Vouvray** (Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé, Muscadet, Chinon) | Sancerre/Pouilly-Fumé are "Essential" |
| **Germany beyond Mosel** (Rheingau, Pfalz, Rheinhessen, Nahe, Baden) | Only Mosel covered |
| **Sparkling beyond Champagne** | Cava, Prosecco, Asti appear only in passing; no Sekt, Cap Classique or Crémant. LO3 is 5 MCQ marks and half of written Q4 |
| Southern France; Greece; Canada; Piemonte beyond Barolo; Tuscany beyond Chianti | Lower individual weight, but all examinable |

Fortified (LO4) is the best-covered minor outcome — Port styles, the full Sherry
range, Rutherglen and Beaumes-de-Venise all appear.
