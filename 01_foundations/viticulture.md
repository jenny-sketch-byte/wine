# Viticulture: Foundations

## Training & trellising
- **Purpose**: control canopy shape/exposure, ease of mechanization, vine vigour management.
- **Key systems to recognize**:
  - **VSP (Vertical Shoot Positioning)**: shoots trained upward in a narrow canopy: good sun exposure and airflow, common in cool/moderate climates, mechanization-friendly.
  - **Bush vine (gobelet)**: no wires, low to the ground, self-shading protects fruit in hot/dry/windy climates (old vine Grenache, Chenin in South Africa).
  - **Head-trained/cane-trained**: traditional, often hand-managed, lower yields.

## Canopy management
- **Purpose**: balance sun exposure and airflow (ripening, disease prevention) against leaf area needed for photosynthesis.
- **Techniques**: shoot positioning; leaf removal (fruit zone), which increases sun exposure and airflow and reduces rot risk but risks sunburn in hot climates; shoot thinning, which reduces overcropping and improves exposure.

## Yield control (why it matters for quality)
- **Pruning** (winter): sets the maximum number of buds/potential shoots: the primary yield control lever.
- **Green harvest (crop thinning)**: removing bunches mid-season to reduce yield and concentrate remaining fruit: costly, used for premium wine.
- **Lower yield ≠ automatically higher quality**, but overcropping dilutes flavour concentration: a standard exam link: yield control → concentration → quality/price tier.

## Irrigation
- Necessary in low-rainfall regions (see `02_regions/other/argentina.md` for the full worked example).
- **Deficit irrigation**: deliberately withholding water at controlled points (commonly post-fruit-set) to limit berry size and concentrate flavour: a precision quality tool where legal/available.
- **Flood/furrow** (cheap, imprecise, promotes vigour/dilution) vs **drip** (precise, targeted, reduces yield, improves concentration).
- Many EU regions restrict or ban irrigation for quality-tier wines: know this contrast with New World regions where irrigation is often essential and normalized.

## Harvest
- **Timing**: driven by target ripeness (sugar/acid balance, phenolic ripeness for reds): picking early preserves acidity/freshness, picking late builds sugar/ripeness/lower acid.
- **Hand harvest**: selective (can leave unripe/diseased bunches), gentler on fruit (whole bunches for carbonic maceration, sparkling base wine), essential on steep slopes: higher cost.
- **Machine harvest**: faster, cheaper, works at night (cooler fruit, preserves freshness): less selective, only viable on suitable terrain (not steep slopes), berries arrive already partly broken (not suitable for whole-bunch methods).

## Disease & pest pressure (mechanism, not just the name)
| Issue | Conditions favouring it | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Powdery mildew (oidium)** | Warm, humid, low light | Coats leaves/fruit with fungus, stunts ripening |
| **Downy mildew** | Warm, wet, humid | Damages leaves, reduces photosynthesis/yield |
| **Grey rot (bunch rot)** | Wet/humid near harvest, damaged skins | Botrytis cinerea spoiling fruit: flavour taint, must be sorted out |
| **Noble rot** | Morning humidity/mist + afternoon sun/wind to dry grapes | Same fungus, but concentrates sugar/flavour without spoiling: used deliberately for sweet wine (Sauternes, Alsace SGN, Tokaji) |
| **Phylloxera** | Root-feeding aphid, native to N. America | Nearly destroyed European viticulture in the 19th century; solved by grafting vinifera onto resistant American rootstock: standard practice today except rare sandy-soil/isolated exceptions (e.g. parts of Chile) |

## Organic / sustainable / biodynamic (know the distinction)
- **Organic**: no synthetic fertilizers/pesticides/herbicides; certified.
- **Biodynamic**: organic base + holistic/lunar-calendar practices, specific preparations (e.g. Demeter certification).
- **Sustainable**: broader umbrella: minimizing environmental impact while allowing some conventional intervention when necessary; often includes social/economic criteria too.

## Frost protection (mechanism matters for "explain" questions)
- Wind machines: mix warmer air above with cold air pooled at vine level.
- Smudge pots/candles: direct heat.
- Sprinklers: water releases latent heat as it freezes, protecting buds inside an ice layer at ~0°C rather than colder ambient temperature.

## Likely exam angles
**"Explain how canopy management/yield control affects grape ripening and wine quality."**

Practice answer (template-based):
> "Leaf removal in the fruit zone increases sun exposure and airflow around the bunches, which improves ripening and reduces the risk of fungal disease in humid conditions. This allows growers to harvest riper, cleaner fruit, resulting in wines with better flavour concentration and fewer green/under-ripe characteristics."
