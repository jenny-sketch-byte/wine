# Alsace

## Climate
- Type: **Cool continental**
- Key factor: **Vosges Mountains** to the west block Atlantic weather systems → rain shadow
- Effects: warm dry summers, cold winters, ~500mm rainfall/year (one of driest regions in France), large diurnal temperature range
- Hazards: low overall (dry autumns reduce rot risk); frost risk in winter

## Soils
- Highly varied (granite, limestone, marl, sandstone) across a narrow strip at the foot of the Vosges
- Contributes to site-specific character seen in Grand Cru distinctions (not core L3 depth: know it exists)

## Key grapes
- Riesling: high acid retention, ages well, reflects the diurnal range especially well
- Gewurztraminer: aromatic, low acid, prone to sugar accumulation
- Pinot Gris: full-bodied, can go dry or sweet
- Muscat: aromatic, usually dry

## Winemaking notes
- Wines almost always labelled by grape variety (unusual for France)
- Little to no oak: stainless steel dominant, to preserve varietal aromatics and acidity

## Styles produced: the KEY exam theme
One grape (esp. Riesling) → full range of sweetness, enabled by climate:

| Style | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Dry | Normal ripeness, picked on schedule |
| Off-dry/medium | Slightly extended hang time; dry autumn allows this safely |
| Vendange Tardive (late harvest) | Extended hang time; diurnal swing concentrates sugar while preserving acid; dry autumn prevents grey rot |
| Sélection de Grains Nobles | Requires botrytis: occasional morning mist (river/valley) + afternoon sun in the right vintages |

## Quality/price factors
- Grand Cru sites (specific soils/aspects) vs village/regional level
- Late harvest and SGN styles = labour-intensive, vintage-dependent → higher price

## Likely exam angles
**"Explain how climatic conditions in Alsace allow Riesling to be made in both dry and sweet styles."**

Practice answer (template-based):
> "Alsace is protected by the Vosges Mountains, causing a rain shadow that results in low rainfall and reduced disease pressure. This means grapes can safely hang on the vine well past normal ripeness without rotting. Combined with a large diurnal temperature range, sugar accumulates gradually while acidity is preserved, allowing producers to pick at different points along this ripening curve: from dry, to late harvest, to botrytized Sélection de Grains Nobles in years when autumn mist triggers noble rot."
