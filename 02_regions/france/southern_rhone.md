# Southern Rhône

## Climate
- Type: Warm, Mediterranean.
- Key factor(s): The Mistral, a strong, dry, cooling wind that funnels down the Rhône valley: reduces disease pressure but can physically damage vines and cause coulure (poor fruit set) at flowering.
- Hazards: Drought, and vintage-to-vintage yield variation driven by the Mistral and dry conditions at flowering.

## Soils
- Famous galets roulés (large, smooth rounded stones), especially in Châteauneuf-du-Pape: absorb heat during the day and radiate it back at night, aiding ripening.
- Otherwise varied: sand, clay, limestone across the wider region.

## Key grapes
- Grenache: the backbone. Thick-skinned, drought-resistant, needs a lot of heat, gives high alcohol, red fruit, and spice, but relatively pale colour and low tannin. Prone to coulure in cool, windy flowering conditions.
- Syrah: adds colour, tannin, dark fruit, and pepper.
- Mourvèdre: adds tannin, structure, and gamey, earthy notes; needs a lot of heat to ripen fully.
- Together: Grenache-Syrah-Mourvèdre (GSM), the region's signature blend.

## Appellation hierarchy
- Côtes du Rhône (regional, accounts for the majority of production) → Côtes du Rhône Villages (stricter yields, higher minimum quality) → named Villages (e.g. Vacqueyras, Gigondas) → Châteauneuf-du-Pape (own appellation, up to 13 permitted grape varieties).

## Winemaking notes
- Blending is the norm, not the exception: rarely single-varietal.
- Old, large-format oak (foudres) is more typical than new small barriques: allows ageing/texture without heavy oak flavour, letting fruit and spice character lead.

## Styles produced
- Mostly full-bodied, spicy, warm-climate reds. Also rosé (a real category here) and some white blends (Grenache Blanc, Clairette, others).

## Quality/price factors
- Old-vine Grenache, low yields, and Châteauneuf-du-Pape's own appellation status are the main quality/price markers over basic Côtes du Rhône.

## Likely exam angles
**"State the stylistic and practical reasons why a winemaker might choose to blend in this region."**

This is the same shape as any blending question, just with this region's grapes swapped in:
- **Stylistic**: Grenache gives alcohol, fruit, and spice but little structure; Syrah and Mourvèdre add colour, tannin, and complexity; the GSM blend continues a long regional tradition.
- **Practical**: Grenache's vulnerability to coulure in windy, cool flowering conditions and the Mistral's own vintage-to-vintage variation mean blending spreads risk across varieties that don't all fail in the same year; Châteauneuf-du-Pape's rules also simply permit blending up to 13 varieties, giving producers room to do it.
