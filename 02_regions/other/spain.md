# Spain (Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Rueda, Rías Baixas, Priorat, Levante)

## Rioja
- **Climate:** Continental, but with a genuinely three-way influence that is worth
  learning precisely, because it explains the sub-zones. The **Cantabrian
  Mountains** to the north shelter Rioja from the worst Atlantic rain. Cool
  **Atlantic** influence reaches Rioja Alta and Rioja Alavesa in the west;
  warmer **Mediterranean** influence reaches Rioja Oriental in the east.
- **Sub-zones:** *Rioja Alta* (higher, cooler, Atlantic-influenced; finer, higher
  acid, more ageworthy), *Rioja Alavesa* (similar, on limestone, north of the
  Ebro), *Rioja Oriental* (formerly Rioja Baja — lower, hotter, drier,
  Mediterranean; riper, higher alcohol, more Garnacha).
- **Grapes:** **Tempranillo** (the backbone: medium-thick skin, early to mid
  ripening, red cherry and plum, developing leather and spice with age),
  **Garnacha** (body and alcohol, contributed largely from the east),
  **Graciano** (acidity and aroma), **Mazuelo** (Carignan; acidity and tannin).
  Whites: **Viura**.
- **Winemaking:** The defining feature is **extended oak maturation, traditionally
  in American oak**, which contributes the coconut, vanilla and sweet-spice
  character strongly associated with the region. Modern producers increasingly use
  French oak for a subtler, more toast-and-clove profile.
- **Ageing law** — the most examinable single table in Spanish wine:

| Term | Red: total ageing | Red: minimum in oak |
|---|---|---|
| **Joven / Generic** | Little or none | None required |
| **Crianza** | 2 years | 1 year |
| **Reserva** | 3 years | 1 year |
| **Gran Reserva** | 5 years | 2 years |

  Note the categories are **minimums in the winery before release**, so the
  producer, not the buyer, carries the cost of holding stock — which is why Gran
  Reserva costs more. Requirements differ for whites and for other regions.

## Ribera del Duero
- **Climate:** **Extreme continental**, on a high plateau at roughly **750–850m**.
  Hot days and very cold nights give a **large diurnal range** that preserves
  acidity and colour while the fruit ripens fully — the classic explanation for the
  region's deep colour and structure.
- The altitude brings a real hazard: **frost in both spring and autumn**, which can
  shorten the growing season at both ends and cut yields.
- **Grape:** **Tempranillo**, known locally as **Tinto Fino** or **Tinta del País**.
  Wines are deeper, more structured and more tannic than Rioja, and generally
  matured in French oak.
- Uses the same Crianza/Reserva/Gran Reserva ageing framework.

## Rueda
- **Climate:** Continental, on the same high Duero plateau; hot days, cold nights,
  low rainfall.
- **Grape:** **Verdejo** — aromatic, with a characteristic fennel and bay-leaf note,
  good acidity, and a slightly bitter finish. Sauvignon Blanc is also permitted.
- **Winemaking:** **Night harvesting is standard**, and is a favourite exam point.
  Verdejo oxidises readily, and picking in the cool of the night keeps the fruit
  cold, limits oxidation before it reaches the winery, and preserves the delicate
  aromatics that define the style.

## Rías Baixas
- **Climate:** **Maritime**, in Galicia in the far north-west — the wettest corner
  of Spain, with well over 1,300mm of rain a year. This makes it completely
  different from the rest of the country.
- The consequence is **fungal disease pressure**, and the vineyard response is the
  examinable detail: vines are trained high on **pergolas** (*parral*), raising the
  canopy well off the damp ground. This **improves air circulation around the
  bunches, allowing them to dry**, reducing fungal disease, and it also prevents
  heat and humidity trapped at ground level from affecting the fruit. The raised
  canopy also allows other crops to be grown beneath in what is a
  smallholding-dominated region.
- **Soils:** Granite, free-draining, which helps in such a wet climate.
- **Grape:** **Albariño** — thick-skinned (useful against rot), high acidity,
  stone fruit and citrus, often with a saline note.

## Priorat
- **Region:** Catalunya. One of only two wines in Spain with the top
  **DOQ/DOCa** classification (with Rioja).
- **Climate:** Hot, dry Mediterranean, but moderated by **altitude** and by the
  surrounding mountains, with cool nights.
- **Soils:** **Llicorella** — a distinctive black slate with mica. It is
  free-draining and forces roots deep in search of water, and it reflects and
  retains heat. Combined with extreme drought and old vines, this produces
  **very low yields**, which is the primary driver of Priorat's concentration and
  its very high prices.
- **Site:** Extremely steep terraced slopes requiring hand work, compounding cost.
- **Grapes:** Old-vine **Garnacha** and **Cariñena** (Samsó), often with Cabernet
  Sauvignon and Syrah. Full-bodied, high alcohol, firm tannin, mineral.

## The Levante (Jumilla, Yecla, Valencia)
- **Climate:** Warm to hot, dry Mediterranean with some continental influence and
  very little rain.
- **Grape:** **Monastrell** — thick-skinned, drought-tolerant, deep colour, high
  tannin, high alcohol, blackberry and plum. Often grown as low-yielding old **bush
  vines**, which shade their own fruit and need no trellising in a dry climate.
- Historically a bulk-wine source, now a value-for-money quality region.

## Spanish quality hierarchy
**Vino** (formerly Vino de Mesa) → **Vino de la Tierra (VdlT)** → **DO** →
**DOCa/DOQ** (Rioja and Priorat only). **Vinos de Pago** sits alongside as a
single-estate designation of high repute.

## Likely exam angles

**"Explain why vines are trained on pergolas in Rías Baixas."**

> "Rías Baixas has a maritime climate with very high rainfall, which creates
> significant fungal disease pressure. Training vines high on pergolas lifts the
> canopy and the bunches away from the damp ground and increases air circulation
> around the grapes, allowing them to dry more quickly after rain and reducing the
> incidence of rot and mildew. The raised canopy also frees the ground beneath for
> other crops, which suits a region of very small landholdings."

**"Explain how the climate of Ribera del Duero influences the style of its wines."**

> "Ribera del Duero sits on a high plateau at 750 to 850 metres with an extreme
> continental climate. Hot, sunny days allow Tempranillo to ripen fully, developing
> deep colour and ripe tannins, while very cold nights slow respiration and preserve
> acidity and aroma. This wide diurnal range produces wines that are deeply
> coloured, structured and high in both tannin and acidity. However, the altitude
> also brings a risk of spring and autumn frost, which can reduce yields."
