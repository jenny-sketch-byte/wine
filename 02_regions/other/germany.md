# Germany

## Climate
- Type: Cool continental, marginal for ripening. Among the coolest wine-producing countries with significant fine wine production.
- Key factor(s): Steep, south-facing valley slopes (the Mosel especially) maximise sun exposure at this latitude; rivers (Mosel, Rhine) moderate temperature and can reflect additional heat onto the slopes.
- Hazards: Frost, poor ripening in weak vintages, rot risk given the marginal climate.

## Soils
- Mosel: slate, which retains and radiates heat, and drains well on steep slopes.
- Rheingau/other regions: varied, including loess and quartzite.

## Key grapes
- Riesling: the signature grape. High acid retention, aromatic, capable of the full sweetness range from bone dry to lusciously sweet, ages very well due to that acid backbone.

## Appellation hierarchy: Prädikatswein, by ripeness at harvest
The six Prädikat categories are ordered by *minimum grape ripeness (sugar level) at harvest*, not by the sweetness of the finished wine: a Kabinett or even an Auslese can be fermented dry.

| Category | Style / sweetness level |
|---|---|
| Kabinett | Lightest Prädikat tier; dry to medium-sweet depending on how far fermentation is taken |
| Spätlese | "Late harvest"; riper than Kabinett; dry to medium-sweet |
| Auslese | Selected, riper bunches; dry to sweet, can show some botrytis |
| Beerenauslese (BA) | Individually selected, botrytis-affected berries; sweet only |
| Trockenbeerenauslese (TBA) | Individually selected, shrivelled/botrytis-concentrated berries; intensely sweet only |
| Eiswein | Grapes frozen naturally on the vine; sweet only, but made without botrytis |

- Sweetness labelling terms (independent of Prädikat): **trocken** (dry), **halbtrocken** (off-dry to medium: literally "half-dry").

## Winemaking notes: Eiswein
- Healthy (not botrytis-affected) grapes are left on the vine into winter and allowed to freeze naturally.
- Grapes are picked and pressed while still frozen: the water content stays behind as ice, so the juice that runs off is extremely concentrated in sugar and acid.
- Fermentation starts and stops naturally, well before all the sugar converts to alcohol, given the very high starting sugar level: results in low alcohol (often 6 to 7% abv) and high residual sugar.
- Oak and malolactic conversion are avoided, to preserve pure varietal, high-acid character.

## Styles produced
- The full range from bone-dry Riesling to Eiswein, all from grape ripeness and winemaking choices on the same base grape: a strong parallel to Alsace's single-grape, full-sweetness-range story, achieved by a different mechanism (ripeness/must-weight categories rather than a rain shadow enabling extended hang time).

## Quality/price factors
- Higher Prädikat tiers (rarer, riper, more labour-intensive to pick) command higher prices; Eiswein and TBA are the most expensive due to the risk and low yield involved in producing them.

## Likely exam angles
**"Describe the process by which Eiswein is produced, and how this affects its style."**

- Grapes freeze naturally on the vine → picked and pressed frozen → water stays behind as ice → juice is highly concentrated in sugar and flavour.
- Fermentation stops naturally before completion, given the high sugar level → low alcohol, high residual sugar.
- No oak, no malolactic conversion → pure, high-acid varietal character carries through.
