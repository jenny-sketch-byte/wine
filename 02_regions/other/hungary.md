# Hungary: Tokaj

## Climate
- Type: Continental, with warm, sunny autumns: essential for noble rot to develop reliably.
- Key factor(s): The Bodrog and Tisza rivers create morning mists (humidity for botrytis to establish) followed by dry, sunny afternoons (to dry the grapes and concentrate them without spoiling): the same morning-mist-plus-afternoon-sun mechanism behind every noble rot region (compare Sauternes, Alsace SGN).
- Hazards: A vintage without the right mist/sun rhythm cannot reliably produce Aszú-quality botrytis.

## Soils
- Volcanic (loess and clay over volcanic bedrock in places): a secondary factor here compared to the climate mechanism that makes noble rot possible at all.

## Key grapes
- Furmint: the backbone grape, thin-skinned and susceptible to botrytis, high natural acidity that balances the finished wine's sweetness and supports long ageing.
- Hárslevelű and a small amount of Muscat are also used.

## Appellation hierarchy
- Historically classified by vineyard site quality (a system with parallels to Burgundy's climats), though the modern exam focus is mainly the Aszú production method itself rather than the site hierarchy.

## Winemaking notes: how Tokaji Aszú is made
1. A base (dry) wine is made first, from healthy, normally-ripened grapes.
2. Separately, Aszú berries (individually selected, botrytis-affected, shrivelled grapes) are harvested by hand, often across multiple passes through the vineyard (called *tries*) to pick only berries that are ready, since botrytis doesn't affect every berry at the same time.
3. The Aszú berries are macerated in the finished base wine.
4. The mixture is pressed to separate the liquid from the solids.
5. Fermentation proceeds slowly, due to the high sugar concentration.
6. The wine is aged in oak, often for several years, adding further complexity.
- **Eszencia**: the term for wine made from just the free-run juice of Aszú berries, without any pressing: extremely rare, extremely concentrated, and can take years to ferment to even a low alcohol level given the sugar concentration.

## Styles produced
- Luscious and sweet, with high acidity balancing the sugar and supporting long ageing. Classic flavours: dried apricot, honey, orange marmalade, nuts, caramel. Full-bodied with a rich texture.

## Quality/price factors
- The number of *puttonyos* (historically, baskets of Aszú berries added per unit of base wine) signals sweetness/concentration level on the label; Eszencia sits above the puttonyos scale as the rarest, most concentrated style.

## Likely exam angles
**"Describe the growing conditions and harvesting of grapes for this style of wine."** (a Tokaji Aszú label)
- Growing conditions: continental climate, warm sunny autumns; the Bodrog/Tisza rivers create the morning-mist-plus-afternoon-sun pattern noble rot needs.
- Harvesting: hand-harvested in multiple *tries* to select only botrytis-affected Aszú berries; long, labour-intensive harvest period.

**"What conditions would you recommend for the long-term storage of this wine?"** See `01_foundations` topic **Service & Storage** in the web app: cool, constant temperature (~10 to 15°C), high steady humidity (~70 to 80%), darkness, on its side if cork-sealed, away from vibration. The same answer applies to any wine intended for long ageing, not just Tokaji.
