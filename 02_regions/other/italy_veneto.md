# Veneto (Valpolicella, Soave)

The Veneto is the single most productive quality region in Italy and carries four
"essential" tasting samples in the Specification. Its exam value is concentrated in
one technique — **appassimento** — which generates a family of four related wines
from the same vineyards.

## Climate
- Type: **Moderate continental**, moderated by two features: the **Alps to the
  north**, which shelter the region from cold northerly weather, and **Lake Garda**
  to the west, whose large body of water moderates temperature.
- The best vineyards are on **hillsides**, where cooler temperatures and better
  drainage preserve acidity and concentrate fruit. Flat valley-floor land is more
  fertile, higher-yielding and produces distinctly plainer wine — a divide the
  *Classico* designations exist to capture.
- Hazards: rain and **fungal disease**, especially relevant because grapes destined
  for drying must arrive at the drying loft completely healthy.

## Soils
Valpolicella Classico's hills are **limestone** with volcanic material; Soave
Classico sits on **volcanic basalt and limestone**, which is the usual explanation
offered for its firmer structure and mineral character relative to flatland Soave.

## Valpolicella: the grapes
- **Corvina** — the principal variety. Thick-skinned, high acid, sour-cherry
  flavour, relatively low tannin. Its thick skins are what allow it to survive
  months of drying without rotting, which is why it is the backbone of Amarone.
- **Corvinone** — a separate variety, once thought to be a Corvina clone.
- **Rondinella** — reliable, disease-resistant, adds colour.
- **Molinara** — high acid, pale; now optional and less used.

## The four Valpolicella styles
This progression is the single highest-value thing to memorise about the region.

| Wine | How it is made | Resulting style |
|---|---|---|
| **Valpolicella** | Normal fermentation of fresh grapes | Light-bodied, high acid, low tannin, fresh sour cherry. Inexpensive, drink young. |
| **Valpolicella Ripasso** | Finished Valpolicella is **re-passed over the leftover skins of Amarone** after that wine has been drained | The skins still hold sugar and unextracted compounds, so a second fermentation adds **alcohol, body, colour, tannin and dried-fruit flavour**. A deliberate middle tier — "baby Amarone". |
| **Amarone della Valpolicella** | **Appassimento**: healthy grapes are dried for around **3–4 months** before fermentation, then fermented **to dryness** | Very concentrated, **high alcohol (15%+)**, full-bodied, raisin, dried fig and cooked fruit. Dry, but so rich it can taste off-dry. |
| **Recioto della Valpolicella** | Same appassimento drying, but fermentation is **stopped early, leaving residual sugar** | **Sweet**, concentrated, still high in alcohol. The historic original style; Amarone was effectively a by-product of it. |

## Appassimento in detail
Grapes are picked slightly early, retaining acidity, and must be **completely
healthy** — any damaged or rot-affected berry will spoil the batch. They are laid
out in single layers on racks or in crates in well-ventilated drying lofts, or in
modern temperature- and humidity-controlled rooms. Over three to four months:

- **Water evaporates**, concentrating sugar, acid and flavour compounds. Weight loss
  is roughly a third.
- Some berries develop **noble rot**, adding complexity in Amarone.
- The main risk is **grey rot**, which is why ventilation, careful spacing and
  humidity control matter so much.

The concentrated must ferments slowly and with difficulty, because the very high
sugar level stresses the yeast. Fermentation can take weeks or months, and for
Amarone it must be pushed all the way to dryness at very high alcohol.

The cost consequences are directly examinable: **drying takes months of space,
handling and loss of volume** — around 100kg of grapes yields far less wine than it
would fresh — so Amarone and Recioto are inherently expensive to produce.

## Soave
- **Garganega** is the principal grape (minimum 70%), with Trebbiano di Soave,
  Chardonnay and Pinot Bianco permitted in the balance.
- Garganega is late-ripening, high in acidity, and relatively neutral, with a
  characteristic almond and white-flower note.
- **Soave Classico** covers the original volcanic hillside zone and is the quality
  heartland; wine from the expanded flatland zone is generally lighter and simpler.
- **Recioto di Soave** applies the same appassimento drying to Garganega to make a
  sweet white — the region's white counterpart to Recioto della Valpolicella.

## Pinot Grigio
**Pinot Grigio delle Venezie DOC** was created in 2017 to bring order and a quality
floor to what had been an enormous volume of generic north-east Italian Pinot Grigio.
Typically light-bodied, neutral to lightly fruity, high volume and inexpensive,
grown on the fertile flat land of the Venetian plain at high yields.

## Law
**DOC** and **DOCG** with the **Classico** and **Riserva** qualifiers. *Classico*
denotes the original, historic — and here, hillside — production zone; *Riserva*
denotes extended ageing. Amarone and Recioto della Valpolicella, and Soave Superiore,
hold DOCG status.

## Likely exam angles

**"Describe the appassimento process and explain how it affects the style of
Amarone."**

> "Healthy grapes are harvested and laid out on racks or in crates in ventilated
> lofts for around three to four months before fermentation. During this time water
> evaporates from the berries, concentrating their sugar, acid and flavour. Because
> the must is so high in sugar, the resulting wine, fermented fully to dryness,
> reaches a very high alcohol level of 15% or more and is full-bodied and
> concentrated, with dried fruit, fig and raisin flavours. Grapes must be free of
> damage and rot before drying, and the lofts must be well ventilated, or grey rot
> will spoil the fruit."

**"Explain how Valpolicella Ripasso differs from Valpolicella in production and
style."**

> "Ripasso is made by passing a finished Valpolicella over the grape skins left
> after Amarone has been drained off. These skins still contain sugar, colour and
> tannin, so a second fermentation takes place, raising the wine's alcohol and
> adding body, colour, tannin and dried-fruit character. The result is fuller and
> richer than standard Valpolicella, which is fermented only from fresh grapes and
> remains light-bodied, high in acidity and low in tannin."
